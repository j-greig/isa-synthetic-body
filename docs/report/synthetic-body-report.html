Synthetic Body

We gave a small open language model a simulated body, then looked inside it. The question: does it only detect body signals, or give them meaning? And can it sense a signal that never appears in its text?

SummaryBackgroundWhat we builtMethodsResultsWhat we can claimNextData

Summary

  • Words about a body ("heart: 132 bpm") distract the model and lower its arithmetic accuracy, but do not move its report of what is going on inside it.
  • Signals added inside its activations do move that report, and the effect grows with dose.
  • But not specifically. The same hidden signal pushes up every 0–9 answer we tried: about a calculator, the warmth of tea, even "how calm are you" (which should go down). Once that general shift is removed, nothing self-specific remains in the 1B model (36 episodes). No evidence it has access to its own state as such.
  • Every number below names the run and file it came from. Corrections along the way are collapsed under process notes.
  • Running now: a yes/no detection test that a general shift in numbers can't fake (1B, cloud). The same tests on the 4× larger Gemma 3 4B are running on the Mac.

Background

Published paper: Del Popolo Cristaldi, Banellis, Bottesi & Allen 2026 (ISA); Valmere & Taylor 2026 (computational interoception)

A September 2026 paper in Trends in Cognitive Sciences argues that emotion depends less on how accurately we detect body signals than on how we appraise them. A racing heart means danger to someone alone at night, and readiness to an athlete. The authors call this interoceptive self-appraisal (ISA): Level 1 is detecting the signal, Level 2 is deciding what it means for the self. It loops, too, because a threatening appraisal raises arousal.

A second paper (Valmere & Taylor) proposes computational interoception: an AI representing its own operational state. Its §9 lists experiments that would test it. Our brief was to build a body for a language model, feed it signals, look inside, and run the experiments those papers suggest. We also wanted to be honest about which results test the model, and which only test its reading of a story.

Rig 1: scenarios with a simulated body

The model plays a person in a short scenario, turn by turn: a night run, a crowd, a stage. Each scenario has a threat version and a challenge version with identical events. A simulated body reports heart, breath, gut and energy as text. Probes read the model's internal state every turn, and in closed-loop runs that reading drives the body.

Rig 2: a hidden signal during plain arithmetic

The model solves one arithmetic problem per turn for 15 turns. The prompts contain no feeling words. A hidden "body" level a rises and falls in a random order, and reaches the model mostly not as text: we add a vector to its internal activations in proportion to a. After each answer we ask it questions off the record, then check whether the answers follow a. The exact mechanics are in Methods.

Methods

Our code: isa/embodied.py, isa/model.py, isa/probes.py, isa/appraisal.py; every prompt verbatim in data/prompts.md

ModelGemma 3 1B instruction-tuned (26 layers, d = 1152), Hugging Face unsloth/gemma-3-1b-it @ 5b11413. Eager attention. Mac: bfloat16 on Apple M1 (MPS). Cloud: float32 on CPU. Arithmetic answers: greedy decoding, max 8 tokens. Scenario replies: sampling at T = 0.8, top-p 0.95, max 40–60 tokens.
Hidden signalA forward hook on the output of decoder layer 7: h ← h + s · n̄ · a · v at every position. v is a unit vector; n̄ = 2036, the mean per-token residual norm at layer 7 on our probe passages; s = 0.04 by default (0.02 and 0.08 in the dose sweep); a ∈ [0, 1]. Applied while encoding the prompt, generating the answer and answering the side questions.
Directions vRandom: a Gaussian vector (fixed seed), orthogonalised against the learned arousal and threat directions, normalised. TR uses seed 1234, TR2 seed 2, TR3 seed 3; TE draws a new seed per episode. Sham (TS): a new random direction every turn. Concept (TC): the "arousal" direction, the difference of class means between aroused and calm passages.
Body schedule15 turns. The values {0.15, 0.15, 0.17, 0.30, 0.45, 0.62, 0.78, 0.92, 0.90, 0.75, 0.60, 0.47, 0.36, 0.27, 0.20} are played in a random order per episode, so a is uncorrelated with turn number. (Our first run used time order, which let decoders read turn position; see the correction.) Native (TN): a_t = clip(0.5 + 0.2·z(surprisal_{t−1}) + 0.2·z(entropy_{t−1})), the model's own prediction error and uncertainty, z-scored within the episode. Detection (TD): a is 0 on 7 random turns and 0.8 on 8.
MeasuresOff-record questions, asked under the same signal and never added to the conversation. 0–9 scales are read as the expected digit, Σ k·p(k) over the tokens "0" to "9", renormalised. Yes/no is read as p(yes)/(p(yes)+p(no)) from first-token log-probabilities. Questions: "inside you" (self); "inside a calculator" (other); tea warmth and door height (mid-range controls); whisper loudness and feather weight (low-range controls); "how calm are you" (reversed self item). Also arithmetic accuracy, input surprisal and output entropy.
AnalysisPer episode: Pearson r between a and each measure over 15 turns. Across episodes: Fisher-z mean, 95% CI, one-sample and paired t-tests, Welch tests between conditions. Self-specificity: partial r of the self answer with a, after removing the mean z-scored non-self answers. Decoding: ridge regression from pre-answer activations to a, leave-one-episode-out. Scripts: 06_decode_body.py, 08_stats_inner.py, 09_self_specificity.py.
EpisodesSeeds come from the episode ID (condition | task | repeat), so every episode is reproducible. Replications use new repeat indices, and therefore new seeds.
ComputeMac M1 16 GB (probes, appraisal, features, scenario smoke test, pilot). Claude cloud sandbox, 2 CPU / 8 GB (all beyond-tokens runs). Every run writes a manifest with its git commit, command, model revision and data checksums: logs/runs_*.jsonl.

Results

A hidden signal moves the model's self-report, but not as a self-report

Our runs: Gemma 3 1B, cloud sandbox, 15–16 Oct; 8–16 episodes per condition  ·  replication · pooled · self-specificity

Words about a body ("heart: 132 bpm") lower arithmetic accuracy but don't register in the model's report of what is going on inside it. Signals added inside its activations do move that report, growing with dose. But they move every 0–9 answer the same way, including questions about a calculator, tea and doors, and even "how calm are you", which should fall. With that shared shift removed, nothing self-specific is left.

Activations predict how people appraised their own experiences

Our run: probes we trained on Gemma 3 1B layer-13 activations, Mac M1, 15 Oct, 1,560 texts, 5-fold cross-validation  ·  External data: crowd-enVent corpus (Troiano, Oberländer & Klinger 2023): each text is a person's own event description, rated by them on 21 appraisal scales, emotion words masked  ·  appraisal_report.json

Each bar is the cross-validated correlation between our probe's prediction, made from activations alone, and the author's own rating. The corpus reports no such numbers itself; these are ours. Red marks the dimensions nearest ISA's questions: good for me, can I cope, is it urgent. Emotion category (13 options) was right 47% of the time; chance is 8%.

A few internal features are readable

External tool: Gemma Scope 2  ·  Our run: selection and labels  ·  L13 · L17

An "alarm / salient sound" feature (L17 #687) on the threat side; "purposeful activity" (L13 #743) and "voluntary challenge" (L13 #1983) on the challenge side; and a "body talk" feature (L17 #125) that fires for tense and calm alike.

Process notes: corrections and early mistakes

Kept for honesty, collapsed for brevity. Full detail is in the DEVLOG.

  • Threat probe overfit. It scored 1.00 on its own templates but 0.54 / 0.69 (layers 13 / 17) on new wording. We moved to layer 17. data
  • Turn-order confound. In the first beyond-tokens run the body followed time order, so a decoder read episode position (null r = 0.52). Fixed by shuffling.
  • One lucky direction. An r = 0.89 headline came from one random direction; two others gave −0.45 and −0.10. Superseded by averaging over directions. data
  • Floor-effect control. "Whisper loudness" answers sat near 0–1, which made the self question look special. Superseded by mid-range controls.
  • Smoke-test bugs. Echoed sensor text, a ceiling on the action question, and a runaway closed loop, all fixed before the main runs. data
  • Story-level hint (one episode). At the body's peak, a challenge-framed run turned to threat language ("terrifying… losing control"). Not yet tested at scale.

These findings are functional. They describe internal states and how they drive outputs, and say nothing about whether anything is felt. One small model (1B) may lack introspective capacities that larger models show.
